The bishop is a strong piece, ranked alongside the rook. Even so, I think it is a hard piece to use well.

The main reason is that **it is a strong piece, yet it cannot move straight forward**.

In this post, I will go through why the bishop is difficult, and what to keep in mind for now.

## It cannot move forward, so it gets harassed easily

The bishop moves only diagonally, so it cannot advance straight ahead.

Because of this, it is easily chased around by the opponent's pawns and other pieces, and it becomes a target of attack from the opening.

If you try to use the bishop up front, it tends to get pushed back. But if you leave it in the back, it does little work and you tend to fall behind.

## It is not very good at defending either

There is also a big difference when it cannot join the attack.

The rook has strong sideways reach, so even when it is not attacking, it often does a fair job in defense.

The bishop, on the other hand, is not very suited to defense. If you cannot put it to work, it becomes **a piece that just gets harassed**.

## You have to watch for a bishop trade

The bishop is a piece that often gets traded early in the game.

So you have to keep checking whether your opponent is aiming for a bishop trade. This is one more thing to think about.

## It is still hard once it is in hand

Once the bishops are traded and the bishop is in your hand, the question becomes where to drop it.

Where should you use your bishop? And where is your opponent trying to drop theirs? Thinking about this is also hard.

## Many of its uses are advanced

I think many of the bishop's uses are advanced ones. For example:

- Threatening to promote the bishop, to restrict how your opponent builds their position. This keeps them from setting up freely.
- Giving up the bishop on purpose, accepting the material loss, to make your attack work.

## Promoting to a horse overcomes the weakness

On the other hand, once the bishop promotes to a horse, it overcomes its weakness of not being able to move forward. That makes promotion very valuable for this piece.

In the opening and middlegame, think of it this way: **if you can promote your bishop while your opponent cannot, that is a big success**.

## For now, do not let your bishop retire

My basic advice is this: if your bishop has retired in your own camp, it is hard to gain the upper hand.

If your bishop still does not reach the enemy camp in the middlegame or later, you need to move it to a square where it works better, or open its diagonal. **A move that puts your bishop to work is a must**.
