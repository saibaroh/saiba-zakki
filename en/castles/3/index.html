Step 3: The Fortress and Choosing Your Castle

Castle Guide  ›  Step 3 / 3

Board color:

Fortress 穴熊

Moves to Build★☆☆☆☆ Durability ★★★★★＋ Balance ★☆☆☆☆ Try Later

Design idea: Tuck the King into the corner and seal it in completely with Gold and Silver.

The Fortress has a design philosophy unlike any other castle. It takes "defend the King solidly" to the extreme: drive the King into the corner (the far edge) and wrap it completely in Gold and Silver. Why is the corner so strong? With the King in the corner, the opponent can only approach from a limited number of directions. Unless they strip away the tightly connected Gold and Silver on the outside, they can't even reach a mating attack — once complete, the King cannot even be put in check. And you can keep adding pieces to the outside for even more durability.

The trade-off is severe. Driving the King all the way to the corner takes many moves, during which you're easily attacked; lose the initiative and the game gets hard. These risks are especially high in static rook vs static rook, so the Fortress is rarely seen there — it mostly appears in opposing setups (static rook vs ranging rook). Because the Gold and Silver lean to the King's side, watch out for Rook/Bishop invasions from the opposite side. It takes some skill to handle, but its late-endgame strength is second to none. Ranging rook players can build a mirrored Fortress on the right.

Choosing Your Castle

Having seen all twelve castles, the natural question is: which one should you use? Here's a simple framework.

Start with your strategy

Castles and strategies are paired. Decide whether you want to play static rook or ranging rook first — that choice narrows your castle options considerably. Pick one side to focus on and find the castle that fits it.

For static rook players

  • Against ranging rook: Boat or Elmo — quick to build and effective
  • Against static rook: Crab, Central King, or Gangi are the standard choices
  • Playing Bishop Exchange: learn the Modern Bishop Exchange formation
  • Prefer a longer game: Yagura is worth exploring later

For ranging rook players

  • Start here: Mino Castle is the default — efficient and upgradeable
  • Want more protection: Progress naturally to High Mino, then Silver Crown
  • Often in mutual ranging rook: Add Double Gold to your toolkit

Don't fixate on a perfect shape

Real games don't always let you reach the ideal formation. The exact positions of your King, Golds, and Silvers will vary depending on how the game develops. Rather than insisting on a specific end-state, think of castles as a direction — something to aim for while staying flexible enough to respond to your opponent's moves. The goal is good King safety, not a diagram-perfect shape.
