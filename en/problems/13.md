## Answer and explanation

**☗B*61**

```board
sfen: k6nl/1sg4r1/pp1g1s1pp/1npp2p2/5p1P1/2PP2P2/1PS2PN1P/L1KGGS3/R7L b BNPbl2p 3
moves: B*6a
caption: Answer
```

This bishop drop has two aims: it attacks the silver on 43, and it threatens to give up the bishop to remove the gold on 72, the key to White’s defense.

### If White defends with ☖S-54

White may answer the threat to the silver with a move like ☖S-54. In that case, Black can close in on White’s king hard with a line like the following, and is winning comfortably.

**☖S-54 → ☗B-72+ → ☖Rx72 → ☗L-93+ → ☖Sx93 → ☗Rx93+ → ☖L*92 → ☗+R-83**

```board
sfen: k6nl/1sg4r1/pp1g1s1pp/1npp2p2/5p1P1/2PP2P2/1PS2PN1P/L1KGGS3/R7L b BNPbl2p 3
moves: B*6a 4c5d 6a7b+ 2b7b 9h9c+ 8b9c 9i9c+ L*9b 9c8c
caption: After ☗+R-83
```

Because the bishop was given up to remove White’s gold, the dragon still has room to escape to 83 even after ☖L*92. That is the point of dropping the bishop first and then sacrificing it.

### The wrong move: ☗L-93+ right away

If Black simply plays ☗L-93+ from the problem position, then after ☖Sx93 ☗Rx93+, ☖L*92 traps the dragon. The gold on 72 covers 83, so the dragon cannot escape with ☗+R-83.

### Another option: ☗N*55

Another option is ☗N*55, a fork. Black is still better after it, but it is less forceful than the correct move.

## Key point

Trading your bishop for the gold that holds White’s defense together, to remove it by force, is a move that comes up often.

A bishop is normally worth more than a gold. But when the trade makes White’s defense collapse like this, you do not need to worry about losing material at all.

If you can see the attack through, go for it with confidence.
