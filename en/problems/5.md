This is a variation on [Problem 4](../4/). White's silver on 27 and horse on 78 have been removed from the board and added to White's hand as a silver and bishop.

The original analysis gives a maximum length of 17 plies. I found this far beyond what I could read in a game; finding it accurately from the starting position struck me as a challenge for an aspiring professional or professional player.

## Answer

The first move is still **R*31**. Interposing a silver or bishop leads to longer variations and additional tactical ideas.

In Problem 4, a gold interposition allowed a rook sacrifice followed by gold drops behind the king. A silver or bishop does not allow that finish and also protects White's gold on 32.

### Silver interposition on 41

Minor variations are omitted, as in the original article.

**R*31 → S*41 → N52+ → Kx52 → S*63 → +Rx63 → Rx41+ → Kx41 → +Bx63 → G*52 → G*51 → Kx51 → S*62 → K42 → Sx53+ → Gx53 → R*41**, mate in 17 plies.

The sequence S*63, +Rx63, Rx41+, Kx41, +Bx63 is especially nice: sacrifice a silver and the rook, then use the horse to capture the defending dragon.

In the original analysis, the interpositions after +Bx63 did not lead to particularly difficult mating patterns.

```board
sfen: l+B2k2+P1/4g1g2/2p1p1npl/pp3NSsp/3+r5/6P2/P4PN1P/4G4/4P1K1L b RSbgsnl6p 1
moves: R*3a S*4a 4d5b+ 5a5b S*6c 6e6c 3a4a+ 5b4a 8a6c G*5b G*5a 4a5a S*6b 5a4b 6b5c+ 5b5c R*4a
caption: Silver interposition: 17-ply sequence
```

### Bishop interposition on 41

The main idea is similar. Again, minor variations are omitted.

**R*31 → B*41 → N52+ → Kx52 → S*63 → +Rx63 → Rx41+ → Kx41 → +Bx63 → R*52 → G*51 → Kx51 → B*62 → K41 → R*51 → K42 → Rx52+**, mate in 17 plies.

```board
sfen: l+B2k2+P1/4g1g2/2p1p1npl/pp3NSsp/3+r5/6P2/P4PN1P/4G4/4P1K1L b RSbgsnl6p 1
moves: R*3a B*4a 4d5b+ 5a5b S*6c 6e6c 3a4a+ 5b4a 8a6c R*5b G*5a 4a5a B*6b 5a4a R*5a 4a4b 5a5b+
caption: Bishop interposition: 17-ply sequence
```

Between the elusive first move, the changing responses to interpositions and the idea of capturing the dragon, there is a lot to enjoy in this difficult problem. I hope you enjoyed it!
