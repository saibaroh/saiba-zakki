Problem 4

[3 dan and above · Tsume] An elusive first move and the battle over interpositions

An unusual mating idea appeared in one of my games, so I wanted to share it.

My personal ratings were four stars out of five for rarity, three for interest, and four for artistry. I think reading it all accurately is a challenge for strong amateur dan players.

In the engine analysis for the original article, only one first move led to mate.

Show answer

Answer

The move that caught my attention was R*31!

If White captures the rook

R31 → Gx31 → N52+ → Kx52 → S43 → K61 → G*52, mate.

Sacrificing the rook draws the gold away, so it can no longer capture the silver dropped on 43.

If White interposes a pawn, lance or knight

Against a pawn drop on 41:

R31 → P41 → N52+ → Kx52 → Rx32+ → L42 → S43+ → K61 → G52, mate.

A lance or knight drop on 41 also fails to stop Rx32+, and the same mating idea applies.

If White interposes a gold

A gold drop calls for a different finish: sacrifice the rook again.

R31 → G41 → Rx41+ → Kx41 → Nx32+ → Kx32 → G31 → K42 → G41, mate.

White can also retreat to 51 instead of capturing the promoted knight; the original article omits that straightforward continuation.

The final position has a tokin and two golds lined up behind the king on the first rank. It is an unusual mating shape.

The original article's engine analysis gives a maximum length of 11 plies. The principal variations shown here finish sooner. The rook sacrifice is easy to overlook, and reading the different interpositions is another challenge.
