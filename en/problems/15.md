Black to move, and Black’s king is in check.

The only ways out of check are ☗K-36 and ☗K-54. Which should you choose?

Squares are written file first, then rank, as labeled on the board (33 = file 3, rank 3).

## Answer and explanation

**☗K-36**

### After ☗K-36, the king escapes

After the correct ☗K-36, one way for White to attack is:

**☗K-36 → ☖+B-46 → ☗Bx46 → ☖+Nx46 → ☗K-26 → ☖B*48 → ☗S*37 → ☖Bx37+ → ☗Nx37**

```board
sfen: ln+Pg3nl/ks3+RR2/pp1+PPp1pp/2p3P2/4sKB2/4+b4/PP3+n1PP/9/L6NL b 3GS4Ps 1
moves: 4e3f 5f4f 3e4f 4g4f 3f2f B*4h S*3g 4h3g+ 2i3g
caption: After ☗Nx37
```

White is just a little short of pieces, and Black’s king escapes.

### ☗K-54 gets mated

After ☗K-54, Black is mated with a line like this:

**☗K-54 → ☖S*45 → ☗K-43 → ☖+B-65 → ☗G*54 → ☖+Bx54**

```board
sfen: ln+Pg3nl/ks3+RR2/pp1+PPp1pp/2p3P2/4sKB2/4+b4/PP3+n1PP/9/L6NL b 3GS4Ps 1
moves: 4e5d S*4e 5d4c 5f6e G*5d 6e5d
caption: After ☖+Bx54 (mate)
```

At first glance, moving up looks safe because more of your own pieces are there. But the reach of White’s pieces in White’s camp, and your own pieces getting in the king’s way, make it a neat mate.

This is a position where it is easy to go wrong if you move on impression. Whether a king gets mated or not really has to be read out carefully.

The moment both options look safe from mate is exactly when you are in the most danger.
