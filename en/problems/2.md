A practical mating problem taken from a real game and featuring a classic tactic.

```board

caption: Problem (Black to move)
hands: show
```

Squares are written file first, then rank, as labeled on the board (34 = file 3, rank 4).

<!-- hint -->

Think about a way to lure the gold on 32 up and out of position.

<!-- answer -->

## Answer and explanation

**☗N\*33 → ☖Gx33 → ☗G\*42**, and that is mate.

```board
sfen: 4lk2l/1+b1ps1g1s/7pp/p3ppp2/8S/1B2P1P1K/1PNP2L2/2G1r4/7+sL b 2GN3Pr2n4p 1
moves: N*3c 3b3c G*4b
caption: Answer
```

The bishop on 86 covers both 31 and 42, so the king has nowhere to run. White's only option is to take with the gold.

Now nothing is guarding 42 anymore. That makes ☗G\*42 work, and it is mate.

**Give up a knight with check to pull the defending gold away.** This tactic comes up often in real games, and it is an important one to know.

The last move can also be ☗G\*31, which is mate as well.

## A bad example: dropping the gold on 42 right away

**☗G\*42 → ☖Gx42**

```board
sfen: 4lk2l/1+b1ps1g1s/7pp/p3ppp2/8S/1B2P1P1K/1PNP2L2/2G1r4/7+sL b 2GN3Pr2n4p 1
moves: G*4b 3b4b
caption: A bad example
```

The gold on 32 simply takes it. The key is to move that gold away with the knight first.

Even dan-level players lose games like this. When you are defending, stay careful until the very end. When you are attacking, keep reading for a mate, even if things look tough!
